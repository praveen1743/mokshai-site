# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **A narrow secret exists.** The CEO says the group has served manufacturing and fabrication for 15 years and has built agents for those industries. Niche workflow know-how is the one durable advantage a small services firm can have, and "AI plus human" suits buyers who cannot afford mistakes.
- **A channel nobody else has.** Moksh Tech (MSP outsourcing), Moksh Estimating and Moksh CAD already sell to the buyers the current site targets. Existing group customers are the cheapest route to the first 100.
- **The repo site already points at a small market:** ConnectWise, Autotask, HaloPSA, NinjaOne, plus MSP calculators and articles.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It enters the most crowded market in the brief.** "AI help for everyone" competes with Morningside, RaftLabs, Leanware, Simform, Lindy and Botsify. Each shows proof (logos, Clutch ratings, case studies, user counts) and several publish prices. Moksh AI shows neither. In a generic market buyers compare like with like, and Moksh AI loses that comparison.
2. **It discards the secret and the channel.** A generic site cannot say "we know fabrication shops" or "we know your PSA". The know-how and sister-company relationships only count if the positioning names the niche; under a "We support" menu they become a footnote.
3. **Nothing is defensible.** A generic consulting site has no proprietary technology, network effect, scale advantage or brand yet, and a copycat could match it within six months. The niche agents are the only candidate for a ten-times advantage, and the generic framing hides them.

Also: the CEO video says manufacturing, the YouTube title says MSPs, the request says everyone. No beachhead has been chosen.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one niche can you lead today, and how many paying clients does the group already have in it? (Unknown.)
- How many sister-company customers could be introduced this quarter, and at what cost each? (Unknown.)
- Which existing agent is clearly better than Lindy, Hatz or a Kaseya/Pax8 built-in for that niche, and how would a buyer see it?
- How would a buyer outside your niche find you rather than Morningside or RaftLabs?
- What stops an MSP platform vendor building your MSP agents into its product?
- What would the generic site have to bring in for you to call it a success?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
PASS on the move as proposed. It becomes FUND IF:
1. The homepage leads with one named niche, one demo-able agent and one real result the company can show.
2. "We support" is a secondary list of adjacent segments, not the headline.
3. A named plan routes sister-company customers to Moksh AI, with cost per lead tracked.
4. A wider audience is added only after the niche has published proof.
