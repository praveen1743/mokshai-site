# Offers lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **Low effort for the buyer.** The CEO's pitch, "You don't have to change anything", with agents fitted to existing systems and people supervising, is the best part of the value equation here.
- **Bounded time to a result.** Fixed-fee discovery, then a fixed-scope pilot, then a monthly run fee gives the buyer a low-risk first step, if the pilot has a stated length.
- **A stack exists.** Five free calculators, a readiness scorecard and a spend audit are cheap entry points. Human review of the last 10% sets it apart from DIY tools like Lindy.
- **The current segments are real markets.** MSPs and manufacturing back offices have named pain and budgets, and the group's 15 years in manufacturing and fabrication gives those buyers a reason to believe.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **"Everyone who needs AI help" is not a market.** The lens picks the market first. A generic audience has no single pain, budget or channel, so it cannot be scored. The move demotes the two segments that can be scored and leads with the one that cannot.
2. **Likelihood falls to near zero.** There are no case studies, logos or testimonials. A specialist wins on knowing the buyer's tools (ConnectWise, HaloPSA, fabrication quoting); a generalist wins only on proof. The generalists in the brief have it: Morningside's logos and 48+ engagements, RaftLabs' 54 client stories, Leanware's Clutch reviews.
3. **It ends in a price fight.** Generic AI agencies publish entry prices (RaftLabs from $10,000, Leanware Sprint 0 at $5–15k, Botsify at $149/month). With no price and no proof, "cheaper" becomes the only point of difference, which makes the offer a commodity.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which buyer has the most pain and money, and where did recent enquiries come from? (Lead sources and win rates per segment are unknown.)
- What one-line dream outcome would a fabrication shop owner and an MSP owner both believe?
- Which manufacturing agents can be demoed or shown as a written result today?
- How long is the pilot, what result does it promise, and what happens if it misses?
- Could you double the price for one segment and still be the obvious choice?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
The vote is PASS. These would move it to FUND IF:
1. Keep one lead segment in the hero. List others under "We support" only once each has its own named pain and a demo or result the company can show.
2. Guarantee the pilot: a defined outcome by a defined date, with a stated remedy if it is missed.
3. Name the stack as one offer (audit, discovery, pilot, run) and publish a starting price or range for discovery.
