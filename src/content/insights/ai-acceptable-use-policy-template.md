An AI acceptable use policy tells staff which AI tools they may use for work, which information must never be entered into them, that a person is responsible for checking anything AI produces, and how to ask for new tools or report a mistake. A good one fits on two pages and is written for the people who will follow it.

Download the [AI acceptable use policy template (Word)](/templates/ai-acceptable-use-policy-template.docx), or copy the text below. Replace everything in [square brackets], delete what does not apply, and have it reviewed before you publish it. This is not legal advice.

## What a good AI usage policy includes

| Section | Why it is there |
|---|---|
| Scope | Makes clear it covers chat tools, AI features in existing software, extensions and agents |
| Approved tools | Gives people a safe option, which is what stops [shadow AI](/what-is-shadow-ai/) |
| Data never to enter | The rule that prevents most real incidents |
| Using AI output | Puts responsibility for accuracy with a person |
| AI features in existing software | Covers the features users can switch on themselves |
| Requests | A quick route to approval reduces workarounds |
| Incidents | Fast, blame-free reporting limits the damage |
| Acknowledgement | A record that each person has read it |

## The template

### [Company name] AI acceptable use policy

Owner: [name, role] · Effective: [date] · Next review: [date, at most 12 months later]

**1. Purpose and scope.** This policy sets out how staff, contractors and anyone working on our behalf may use AI tools at work. It covers chat assistants, AI features inside software we already use, browser extensions, transcription and note-taking tools, and AI agents connected to our systems. It applies on company devices and on personal devices used for work.

**2. Approved tools.** Use only the tools below for work, signed in with your company account. Personal or free accounts are not approved for company or client information.

| Tool | Approved for | Account |
|---|---|---|
| [e.g. Microsoft 365 Copilot] | [Drafting, summarising internal documents] | Company account only |
| [e.g. ChatGPT Business / Claude Team] | [Drafting, research with no client data] | Company workspace only |
| [Tool] | [Use] | [Account] |

**3. Information you must never enter into an AI tool.**

- Passwords, API keys, MFA codes or any other credentials.
- Client data, unless the tool is approved for client data in section 2 and the client contract allows it.
- Personal data about staff, clients or the public beyond what the task strictly needs.
- Payment card, bank, health or government ID information.
- Contracts, pricing, source code or other confidential material in any tool not approved for it.

**4. Using what AI produces.**

- You are responsible for anything you send, publish or act on, whether or not AI helped produce it.
- Check facts, figures, names, links and code before you use them. AI tools state wrong things confidently.
- Do not let AI make final decisions about people, such as hiring, discipline, credit or pricing for an individual. A person decides.
- Tell the recipient when a client deliverable was substantially produced by AI, if the client contract or [Company name] guidance requires it.

**5. AI features inside software we already use.** Many tools now include AI features that any user can switch on. Do not switch on a new AI feature, connector or plug-in that reads company data without approval from [role].

**6. Asking for a new tool.** Ask [role / channel] before you use any AI tool not listed in section 2. Include what you want to use it for and what information it would see. We aim to answer within [number] working days.

**7. Mistakes and incidents.** If you entered information you should not have, or an AI tool produced something that caused harm, tell [role / channel] the same day. Reporting quickly matters more than blame.

**8. Breaches.** Breaking this policy may lead to loss of access to AI tools and, for serious or repeated cases, action under [disciplinary policy].

**9. Acknowledgement.** I have read this policy and will follow it. Name, signature, date.

## How to roll it out

1. **Fill in the approved tools first.** If you do not know what is in use, run the discovery steps in the [shadow AI guide](/what-is-shadow-ai/) and the [AI subscription audit](/tools/ai-spend-audit/).
2. **Explain it in a short team meeting.** Ten minutes covering why it exists, what is approved and how to ask for more does more than an email.
3. **Collect acknowledgements.** A form or your HR system is enough.
4. **Show people how to use the approved tools well.** A policy without training leaves people unsure, and unsure people go back to their personal accounts. Role-based training is part of our [implementation and training](/services/ai-implementation-training/) service.
5. **Put it in the register.** The policy is one of the fixed rules in your [AI governance framework](/ai-governance-framework-small-business/), and its review date belongs in the quarterly review.

## Common mistakes

- **Only listing bans.** People still need to get work done. Name at least one approved tool.
- **Writing for lawyers.** If staff cannot remember the three main rules, it will not be followed.
- **Forgetting built-in AI.** Meeting recorders and AI features in CRMs and help desks read the same data as a chat tool.
- **No owner.** Without a named person to ask, requests go nowhere and the list goes stale.
